Course

Study all levels, from your first words to fluent debate

The course is organized by CEFR level, from A1 to B2. Each level lists its grammar focus and vocabulary focus. Course content stays in English regardless of your interface language, since it's what you're here to practice.

🚀
Start the course
A1 to B2: 300 interactive lessons, placement test, review, printable sheets and final exams
By the end of A1 you can introduce yourself, ask simple questions, and understand short, everyday phrases spoken slowly.
✏️ Grammar Focus
  • The verb "to be" (am / is / are)
  • Present simple, affirmative and negative
  • Articles: a, an, the
  • Singular and plural nouns
  • Subject pronouns and possessive adjectives
💬 Vocabulary Focus
  • Greetings and introductions
  • Numbers, days, and months
  • Family members
  • Colors and everyday objects
  • Basic food and drink
By the end of A2 you can describe your routine, talk about the past, and handle simple exchanges while shopping or traveling.
✏️ Grammar Focus
  • Past simple, regular and irregular verbs
  • Present continuous
  • Comparatives and superlatives
  • There is / there are
  • Countable and uncountable nouns
💬 Vocabulary Focus
  • Daily routines
  • Food and restaurants
  • Travel and directions
  • Weather and seasons
  • Clothes and shopping
By the end of B1 you can talk about experiences, give opinions, and cope with most situations while working or traveling in English.
✏️ Grammar Focus
  • Present perfect vs. past simple
  • Future forms: will, going to, present continuous
  • Modal verbs: should, must, might, can
  • First conditional
  • Basic relative clauses (who, which, that)
💬 Vocabulary Focus
  • Work and studies
  • Opinions and agreement/disagreement
  • Health and the body
  • Technology and social media
  • Phrasal verbs (common set)
By the end of B2 you can follow complex arguments, write clear detailed text, and interact fluently with native speakers.
✏️ Grammar Focus
  • Passive voice, all tenses
  • Reported speech
  • Second and third conditionals
  • Relative clauses, defining and non-defining
  • Modal verbs of deduction (must have, can't have)
💬 Vocabulary Focus
  • Media and current affairs
  • Environment and sustainability
  • Abstract ideas and feelings
  • Common idioms and collocations
  • Formal vs. informal register
